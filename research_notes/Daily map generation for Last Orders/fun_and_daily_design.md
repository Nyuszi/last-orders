## 1. PCG quality evaluation: expressive range, diversity, quality-diversity, "oatmeal", generator vs designer control

### Takeaway
Judge the generator, not single maps. Plot thousands of seeds on 2-3 designer-chosen metrics (expressive range analysis), then select for maps that are perceptually unique, not just mathematically different. MAP-Elites/constrained MAP-Elites is the standard way to guarantee variety across cells of a behavior grid. The best-known procedural golf game (Desert Golfing) uses hand-authored constraints layered on a generator.

### Cited Findings
- Expressive range analysis was introduced by Smith and Whitehead "as a means of visualizing generative spaces and assessing the variety and quality of levels produced by generators". Their Mario-style analysis used metrics such as linearity and leniency (and density) to show which kinds of levels a generator tends to produce and which it rarely or never produces. — [Level Generation with Constrained Expressive Range (arXiv 2504.05334, 2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.05334)
- Summerville and Mateas used corner plots: each metric's distribution on the diagonal, pairwise metric scatter/heatmaps off-diagonal. Guzdial et al. used expressive range visualization during development of *That's Me TV* to tune mechanics until the output matched design goals. — [arXiv 2504.05334](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.05334)
- Angry Birds level generators (Stephenson & Renz, AIIDE 2016) characterised generated levels with frequency, linearity, density, leniency, and playability metrics. This is a direct physics-game analogue of ERA. — [Stephenson & Renz AIIDE16 (via search summary)](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~jrenz/papers/stephenson-renz-aiide16.pdf)
- Kate Compton's "10,000 Bowls of Oatmeal" problem: outputs can each be mathematically unique yet feel samey. Commentators summarise her distinction between *perceptual differentiation* (you can tell two outputs apart at a glance, but the difference isn't significant) and *perceptual uniqueness* (the output is memorable, "a main character versus a face in the crowd"). Suggested remedies: constraints that make variation noticeable, grouping outputs into types/archetypes, identifying the characteristics the audience actually cares about, and hand-curating after generation. Differentiation alone is fine for background elements (sand grains, waves). — [CMU 60-212 course reading responses (2020)](https://courses.ideate.cmu.edu/60-212/f2020/category/01/01-reading/index.html); [CMU 60-210 reading responses (2021)](https://ems.andrew.cmu.edu/2021s/60210a/category/deliverables-02/02-reading/index.html). Compton's original post was not retrieved, so this is secondary.
- A July 2026 Boris the Brave post ("Oatmeal Spice") argues that handling variation and interestingness is now the core skill of procedural generation, especially as GenAI takes over much of the technical side. — [Boris the Brave, 2026-07-05](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2026/07/05/oatmeal-spice/) (summary only, not read in full)
- MAP-Elites splits the population into cells by values along several behavioural dimensions and keeps the best individual (elite) per cell, giving a grid of diverse, high-quality levels instead of one optimum. The Evolutionary Dungeon Designer adds an *interactive* constrained MAP-Elites, where designers change the dimensions and their edits feed back into the search (mixed-initiative). — [Alvarez et al., arXiv 1906.05175 (2019)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.05175)
- Constrained MAP-Elites combines MAP-Elites with FI-2Pop (feasible/infeasible populations). In one MiniDungeons 2 study, map quality was defined by simplicity: simpler maps scored higher. A cited advantage of QD-PCG is that the designer doesn't have to hand-code a complex fitness function as in single-objective search. — [MAP-Elites search summary: arXiv 1906.05175; arXiv 2102.12463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12463)
- Other QD level work: PlayMapper (MAP-Elites for Super Mario Bros with player-based and level-based features), MAP-Elites in VAE latent space blending five platformers (Sarkar & Cooper, FDG 2021), and CMA-ME for continuous spaces (Zelda, Pacman). — [arXiv 2102.12463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12463); [arXiv 2007.05674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.05674v4)
- Desert Golfing: holes are "procedurally generated, but with human orchestrated constraints that slowly introduce new gameplay aspects". Reverse Shot describes "a computer algorithm working within certain parameters" plus "clearly, human orchestration". Justin Smith on why he generated holes: "How many golf holes could I design by hand before going loco? Not many." — [Wikipedia: Desert Golfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_Golfing); [Reverse Shot](https://reverseshot.org/features/1927/desert_golfing); [Game Developer: 7 questions for Justin Smith](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/7-questions-for-i-desert-golfing-i-creator-justin-smith)
- Cut the Rope's Ropossum (AIIDE) pairs a physics engine with an evolutionary framework and an AI agent that checks solvability and optimises for playability. An earlier version had each level played 10 times by a random player because heuristics alone did not guarantee playability. A later projection-based method was built for fast, real-time designer feedback. — [Ropossum, AIIDE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12611); [ITU: Evolving playable content for Cut the Rope through simulation](https://pure.itu.dk/en/publications/evolving-playable-content-for-cut-the-rope-through-a-simulation-b/); [Shaker 2015 projection paper](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/files/226436460/Shaker2015Projection.pdf)

### Inferences
- For Last Orders, a practical ERA setup is to generate around 10,000 seeds offline and compute per-map metrics with a headless physics solver, such as: (a) *leniency*, the fraction of a random/jittered shot sample that scores; (b) *required-precision band*, the angular/power width of the winning shot region; (c) *mean bounces on solution paths*; (d) *mechanic usage vector*, i.e. which of rail-bank, obstacle, slapping arm, hazard hat, trick-shot and spin the found solutions use; (e) *path linearity*, solution path length divided by the straight-line distance to the target. Corner-plot these to see holes (never-generated map types) and clumps (oatmeal).
- Use a MAP-Elites archive offline as a *calendar bank*. Make behaviour dimensions the things players perceive, e.g. "signature mechanic" (categorical) × "difficulty band" (precision-width quantile). Each day draws from a different cell under a rotation rule (no same signature mechanic two days running), so variety across days is structural, not luck. The quality/fitness is a playability-and-interest score (section 6). Constrained MAP-Elites/FI-2Pop handles the hard constraints (solvable, no impossible circles, no physics glitches) separately from quality.
- Seeded-by-UTC-date determinism (everyone gets the same map) is compatible with this: seed → archive index, or seed → generate-then-repair-then-verify, and reject/regenerate deterministically until the verifier passes.
- "Generator vs designer" control: the Desert Golfing model (algorithm plus hand-orchestrated parameter schedules introducing aspects over time) and EDD's mixed-initiative MAP-Elites suggest an authored *weekly schedule template* (which mechanic is featured, intended difficulty) that parameterises the generator. Add a manual veto/curation pass over the upcoming week's maps if time allows.
- To counter oatmeal, give each day a named archetype ("The Long Bank", "Hat Trick", "Slap Happy"). Perceptual uniqueness comes from a memorable dominant feature, not from small coordinate jitter.

- Original Smith & Whitehead (2010) metric formulas were not retrieved.

## 2. Fun in physics/aiming puzzles: aha, near-misses, skill vs luck, flow, readability, telegraphing, juice

### Takeaway
Near-misses motivate replay, but only when the player feels in control: a strong fit for a skill-based aiming game with a "Took N tries" share. Puzzle "aha" requires an insight the player can discover, which in aiming games means a readable intended route (e.g. a visible bank line). Juice is widely recommended but empirically mixed: do it for polish, not as a substitute for interesting maps.

### Cited Findings
- Clark et al. (2009, *Neuron*): near-misses were rated less pleasant than full misses but *increased the desire to play*. This effect was restricted to trials where the subject had personal control over arranging the gamble. Near-misses recruited striatal and insula circuitry that also responds to wins (fMRI, n=15 per press release). — [PMC2658737 (Clark et al. 2009)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2658737); [EurekAlert press release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/653015); [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/gamblers-brains-almost-counts)
- The lead author noted that in random games (slots, roulette) near-misses do not signal future success. In skill games they genuinely do, which is why the motivational response evolved. — [Science News / press summary](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/gamblers-brains-almost-counts)
- "Juice It or Lose It" (Jonasson & Purho, GDC 2012): a mediocre Breakout became "instantly fun" after adding flash, screen shake, floating text, sound and particles. Juice is defined as redundant feedback where one player action triggers multiple non-functional reactions. — [Game feel (Wikipedia)](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_feel); [roblog summary](https://roblog.co.uk/2024/03/juicy-games/)
- Empirical evidence for juice is mixed. Juul & Begy (2016) found null results in an exploratory study of juicy feedback. Hicks et al. (2018, 2019) proposed and tested a framework with 40 and 32 participants (results not retrieved). A 2014 critique argued polish can cost immersion. — [search summary: Hicks et al. 2019, doi 10.1145/3311350.3347171](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F3311350.3347171); [core.ac.uk PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/163027665.pdf)
- The "aha moment" is described as the most important feeling in puzzle design (third-party essay, not Mark Brown). GMTK's video on Jonathan Blow says Blow's philosophy is about "better, and more honest puzzles", and that ideas emerge from exploring the mechanics during development. — [enezio "Epiphany" notes](https://enezio.notion.site/Epiphany-3129fa92c6c6806aa0dfd1decb06d018); [GMTK: How Jonathan Blow Designs a Puzzle (transcript)](https://amara.org/subtitles/LaGKzzSXnuQl/en-gb/1/download/How%20Jonathan%20Blow%20Designs%20a%20Puzzle%20%20Game%20Makers%20Toolkit.en-gb.txt)
- Mark Brown, designing *Mind Over Magnet*, reportedly used a mechanic-interaction matrix, building puzzles around interactions between each pair of mechanics (third-party write-up). — [enezio "How to create many puzzles"](https://enezio.notion.site/How-to-create-many-puzzles-2f29fa92c6c68005a931d103ffb0bebe)
- Walkabout Mini Golf's Don Carson (Passport: Hollywood) says the team is often willing to sacrifice "good shots" for "just to see what happens if we knock it against that wall": playful physical interaction is valued over pure optimal-shot play. — [Walkabout search summary; Meta blog](https://www.meta.com/blog/walkabout-mini-golf-myst-launch-meta-quest-2/), originally reported via [UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/walkabout-mini-golf-passport-hollywood-course/)

### Inferences
- **Near-miss design:** Last Orders is a skill game, so near-misses are honest signals and a good motivator. Make them *visible and legible*: show how close the glass stopped to the circle ("2 cm short!"), ghost-trail the last shot, and let the retry start instantly. Avoid fake near-misses (randomness that makes it look close): the Clark finding depends on perceived control.
- **Skill vs luck:** keep physics deterministic (same input gives same outcome, fixed timestep) so that retries are learning, not rerolls. The timed slapping arm is the main timing-skill element. Its period should be constant and telegraphed (wind-up animation) so players can learn it.
- **Aha in aiming games:** the insight is usually "the direct line is blocked or hazardous, but there's a bank/trick route". The generator should produce maps where the *naive straight shot fails* (blocked or hits the hazard hat) but a discoverable alternative exists. Readability: rails visibly bouncy, hazard hat visibly dangerous, slap-arm wind-up visible before it swings.
- **Flow (Csikszentmihalyi, general knowledge, not retrieved here):** challenge should match skill. For a daily game that means a weekly ramp (section 4) plus within-day difficulty from multiple customers/circles of increasing difficulty.
- **Juice:** prioritise feedback that carries information (bounce sparks on rails, a "clink" on trick-shot collisions, slow-mo on a glass settling near the circle edge, foam slosh). The 2012 talk is persuasive; the academic evidence says juice is not guaranteed fun on its own.
- **Mechanic-pair matrix:** enumerate pairs (bank × hat, spin × slap-arm, trick-shot × obstacle…) and make sure the calendar covers each pair over a few weeks. This gives variety plus the "one twist per day" feel.

## 3. Minigolf hole design principles (digital and real)

### Takeaway
Walkabout's designers treat each hole as a short story with a twist on a familiar hole. Pars stay low, the final hole is special, and playful "what happens if I hit that" interactions matter more than optimal-shot purity. This supports "one signature feature per map" and a showpiece trick/bonus.

### Cited Findings
- Walkabout's lead level designer Henning Koczy: "level design is story design"; a single three-stroke hole can carry its own story beats (Upside Town interview). — [Meta blog: Upside Town](https://www.meta.com/blog/upside-town-walkabout-mini-golf-course-launch/)
- Myst course: holes had to feel like intricate Myst-style puzzles "while still keeping the pars low and having fun and replayability". "Many feel like more traditional mini golf holes with a unique twist, while a few are full-on puzzles." — [Meta blog: Walkabout Mini Golf: Myst](https://www.meta.com/blog/walkabout-mini-golf-myst-launch-meta-quest-2/)
- In Walkabout, the 18th hole "is always designed to be special". A Hole 17 staircase room was used to prove ceiling golf was possible. — [Meta blog (Walkabout course launches)](https://www.meta.com/blog/upside-town-walkabout-mini-golf-course-launch/)
- Don Carson (ex-Imagineer, Senior Art Director) designs environments so that every corner reveals a new setting and hole (theme-park reveal design). UploadVR published his "Principles of course design" from a 45-minute talk (not retrievable here). — [Meta developer blog: Don Carson interview](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/blog/walkabout-mini-golf-don-carson-interview-20000-leagues/); [UploadVR: principles of course design](https://www.uploadvr.com/principles-of-course-design-in-walkabout-mini-golf/)
- Desert Golfing: no menus, no restart, no lose condition; "you cannot go back and do it better… Those strokes are on your score forever." The permanence makes each shot weighty. — [Medium: Alec Thomson interview (2014)](https://medium.com/@alecthomson/the-end-of-desert-golfing-and-other-stories-from-mysterious-canadian-justin-smith-5179952261ac); [Reverse Shot](https://reverseshot.org/features/1927/desert_golfing)
- What the Golf? (Triband): game director Anne Clausen's thesis is that good design can make players "burst out laughing" through surprise, e.g. a flag that suddenly runs away from the ball. Levels end with on-screen words that sometimes carry the punchline. Creative director Tim Garbos: "We spent around three months just coming up with crazy ideas and never really stopped." Critics note some levels drift so far from golf that the central joke is briefly lost. — [Ing.dk (2022-09-06)](https://ing.dk/artikel/developer-behind-award-winning-golf-game-good-design-can-get-player-burst-out-laughing-surprise); [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/what-the-golf-putts-around-with-physics/); [GameSpot review](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/what-the-golf-review-swing-and-a-hit/1900-6417335/); [Game Developer: Road to the IGF](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-triband-s-i-what-the-golf-i-)
- Golf Peaks (Afterburn, Łukasz Spierewka; 2019): card-based golf puzzle in which you choose a movement card and swipe a direction. The ball won't drop into the hole as it passes over, which turns the hole into a puzzle about exact stopping. 108 levels in nine worlds of twelve. The developer admitted on itch.io that one level "was a bit of a difficulty spike" in hindsight. — [Wikipedia: Golf Peaks](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Golf_Peaks); [itch.io dev comments](https://afterburn.itch.io/golf-peaks/comments?after=51); [Pixelpoppers review](https://pixelpoppers.com/review/golf-peaks/)

### Inferences
- Model each daily map like a Walkabout hole: familiar base (slide to circle) plus **one unique twist** (the day's signature mechanic) and a short arc (setup customer → twist customer → showpiece "last orders" customer, mirroring the "18th hole is special" rule).
- "Par" for Last Orders: compute par from the solver as the minimum pours needed by a competent bot, maybe plus a buffer. Show "under par/par/over par" in the share text alongside tries. Keep par low (as Walkabout does) so most players can finish.
- Reward playful interactions (trick shots off a missed glass) with bonus feedback even when suboptimal, per Carson's "see what happens" philosophy.
- Desert Golfing's permanence argues for counting tries prominently and making the *first* attempt special ("First try" badge), while still allowing retries for accessibility.
- What the Golf? shows that surprise and comedy are a legitimate "signature feature". An occasional joke map (e.g. the target circle slides away, a customer catches the glass) can be memorable daily content, but anchor it to the core slide-to-circle verb so the joke isn't lost.
- Golf Peaks' rule that the ball must *stop* in the hole is analogous to Last Orders' glass needing to *settle* in the circle. Stop-precision, not just path, is a key difficulty dial. Also watch for single-map difficulty spikes in an otherwise smooth ramp (the solver's precision-band metric can flag them).

## 4. Daily puzzle design lessons (Wordle, NYT, Desert Golfing, weekday ramps)

### Takeaway
The daily format works because of scarcity (once a day), a shared identical puzzle, and a spoiler-free share artifact that tells the story of the attempt. Curated difficulty (Wordle's trimmed answer list; NYT's Monday→Saturday ramp, measured by solve times and completion rates) matters more than raw novelty.

### Cited Findings
- Josh Wardle borrowed the once-a-day limit from the NYT word games he was playing. He argued the single shared daily puzzle created the social dimension. — [Game Developer: GDC 2022 recap](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/josh-wardle-reflects-on-the-the-unconventional-road-to-wordle-s-success)
- The emoji share grid was invented by players. Users were posting emoji blocks on Twitter, and Wardle then built it in. Sharing didn't exist until mid-December 2021. Wardle: when you play alone "you go on a journey", and the grid "becomes a way in which you can share that story really easily with others". — [Game Developer GDC 2022](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/josh-wardle-reflects-on-the-the-unconventional-road-to-wordle-s-success); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2256733); [Thumbsticks](https://www.thumbsticks.com/wardle-on-wordle-simple-word-game-global-phenomenon/)
- Wordle originally had a much harder word list. Wardle curated the answer list down to words people actually know (GDC 2022). — [Axios, 2022-03-25](https://www.axios.com/2022/03/25/wordle-wordlist-josh-wardle-gdc)
- NYT crossword: designed to increase in difficulty Monday (easiest) → Saturday (hardest). Will Shortz deliberately "steepened the slope of difficulty" when he took over in 1993. Sunday is pitched around Wednesday/Thursday ("Thursday-plus" per some sources; sources disagree). — [Wikipedia: NYT Crossword](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times_crossword)
- Difficulty can be measured from player data: Puzzazz's 2016 Difficulty Index used anonymised solve times for every Times crossword since July 2015 and largely confirmed the ramp. Shortz uses completion rate ("Fewer people can do the Fridays"). — [GeekWire 2016](https://www.geekwire.com/2016/hard-new-york-times-crossword-startup-finds-answer-first-ever-data-analysis/); [Puzzazz Difficulty Index](https://www.puzzazz.com/puzzle-difficulty-index)
- NYT Connections (editor Wyna Liu): puzzles built from word clusters, refined in spreadsheets, with deliberate "decoys" (red herrings). "More overlap makes a harder puzzle." — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/connections-puzzle); [Wikipedia: Wyna Liu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyna_Liu)
- Desert Golfing (no restart, permanent strokes) shows the "one-shot" extreme of commitment. — [Reverse Shot](https://reverseshot.org/features/1927/desert_golfing)
- Roguelike daily runs: Spelunky's Daily Challenge gives everyone the same seeded adventure with one attempt ("If you die, you'll have to wait until the next day!") and a per-day leaderboard. It reportedly grew out of a friends' ritual of playing only one run per day. Critics note the one-chance rule adds pressure. Cruel Dungeon instead ranks only the first run, then allows unlimited practice. A developer write-up argues a shared fixed seed (the calendar date) is essential for a fair board, since without it runs can only be compared statistically. — [Spelunky Wiki: Daily Challenge](https://spelunky.fandom.com/wiki/Daily_Challenge_Mode_(HD)); [Game Developer: The understated genius of the Spelunky daily](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-understated-genius-of-the-i-spelunky-i-daily-challenge); [Game Developer: The 24-hour ticket, examining daily runs](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-24-hour-ticket-examining-daily-runs-); [Kotaku: More games need daily challenge modes](https://kotaku.com/more-games-need-daily-challenge-modes-1740109783); [Product Hunt: Cruel Dungeon](https://www.producthunt.com/products/cruel-dungeon/makers); [DEV: date as seed](https://dev.to/yurukusa/5-lines-of-code-that-made-my-roguelike-worth-playing-every-day-3klj)
- Bandle (Johann Levy): an audio song-guessing daily with 100,000+ daily players; Levy credits machine learning, "Reddit friends", and a loyal community for growth. Globle (Abe Train, 2022) deliberately signalled Wordle lineage with its "-ble" name. CineNerdle's creator says seeing Framed's popularity inspired his own movie daily. This is evidence that daily games spread by recognisable format families. — [Berklee Online Take Note: Bandle](https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/bandle-creator-johann-levy-on-the-music-quiz-100k-daily-players-love/); [Hey.gg: Globle / Trainwreck Labs](https://hey.gg/blog/trainwrecklabs); [Hey.gg: CineNerdle](https://hey.gg/blog/cinenerdle)
- Zach Gage (Puzzmo): "Difficulty and expectations are not really linked." Designers must make sure players grasp each concept before facing a puzzle at the right level (bottom-up). He spent almost a year tuning TypeShift's difficulty though the core concept barely changed. — [Game Developer: Zach Gage on newspaper games](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/puzzmo-co-creator-zach-gage-on-building-newspaper-games-that-can-last-forever); [Game Developer: TypeShift difficulty curve](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-zach-gage-fine-tuned-the-difficulty-curve-of-i-typeshift-i-)

### Inferences
- **Weekday ramp for Last Orders:** Monday = generous leniency/wide precision band, single mechanic; Saturday = narrow band, two-mechanic interaction; Sunday = "special" (bigger saloon, showpiece trick). Calibrate with the solver's precision-band metric before launch, then *recalibrate from real tries-to-success data* (the Puzzazz/Shortz approach: median tries and completion rate per day).
- **Decoys:** the aiming-game equivalent of Connections' red herrings is a tempting direct line that fails (hazard hat or slap-arm in the way) while a bank route exists. Overlap of tempting routes gives a harder puzzle.
- **Share text:** follow Wordle, a spoiler-free story artifact. Example: day number + per-customer glyphs (🍺 scored first pour, 🎱 bank shot, 🎩 hat hit, 💥 slapped, ✨ trick shot) + tries ("First try"/"Took 4 tries") + par. It should tell the journey without revealing the map layout.
- **Retries vs one-shot:** offer unlimited retries but score and share the *first attempt* and the tries count, which combines Desert Golfing's weight with accessibility. This is the Cruel Dungeon model (first run ranked, practice after) and avoids Spelunky's one-chance pressure.
- **Teach before testing (Gage):** introduce each mechanic on an easy day before it appears in a hard combination. For example, the first-ever hazard-hat map should be a Monday with a forgiving band. The weekly schedule template should encode "first appearance" days.
- **Seed by UTC date** for fairness of tries comparison (the roguelike daily-run lesson). Also make sure the build/physics version is pinned per day so a mid-day deploy can't change the map.
- Curate answer space like Wordle: reject maps that are technically solvable but need pixel-perfect shots (precision band below a floor), especially early in the week.

### Gaps
- Framed's own design and Polytrack/daily racing ghosts were not found. Bandle/Globle findings are about growth and naming, not mechanics.
- No retrieved data on streak mechanics' retention effect (e.g. NYT streak stats).

## 5. Reddit/Devvit daily games and hackathon context

### Takeaway
Reddit has run a sequence of game hackathons (2024 Games & Puzzles, 2025 Hack Reddit / Kiro / Fun and Games with Devvit Web, early-2026 Daily Games Hackathon, mid-2026 Games with a Hook). They emphasise recurring/daily content, community/UGC, and "Delightful UX". Reddit's own flagship games (Pixelary, Riddonkulous, Sword & Supper) lean on community participation and UGC.

### Cited Findings
- **Likely the user's event: "Reddit's Game of the Day Hackathon" (Nov–Dec 2026).** Devpost deadline Dec 8, 2026 6:00pm PST. Judging Dec 9–16, 2026; winners on or around Dec 17, 2026. Stage Two criteria are *equally weighted*. A third-party summary lists them as delightful UX, polish, how Reddit-like/community-minded it is, and whether it creates fresh recurring content. The Devpost page says "Reddit-y" theming does **not** mean the game must be about Reddit, karma, subreddits or Snoo. Judges score from the **demo link**, so the demo post must be self-explanatory. Reported prizes: $15k best game, $6k best ported game, ten $1k honourable mentions. ## 6. Automatic proxies that correlate with player-rated fun

### Takeaway
There is solid precedent that simulation-computed aesthetic measures can predict human preference: Browne's Ludi found 16 criteria that predicted player rankings and evolved the commercially published Yavalath. For physics puzzles, published work verifies *playability* via simulation agents but rarely validates *fun* with players. Treat any metric set as a hypothesis to calibrate against real tries/completion data after launch.

### Cited Findings
- Browne & Maire (2010) "Evolutionary Game Design" / Ludi: criticism module measures games through self-play against aesthetic criteria. Correlating player rankings with measurements identified **16 key criteria predicting game quality**. A later run evolved 1,389 games from 79 seeds, with 19 deemed viable, confirming aesthetic measures predict player interest. Yavalath, a Ludi-evolved game, was commercially published (won 2012 Humies). — [Browne & Maire, Evolutionary Game Design (academia.edu)](https://www.academia.edu/80415661/Evolutionary_Game_Design); [Maastricht CRIS](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/evolutionary-game-design); [SIGEVO: Humies 2012](https://sig.sigevo.org/article4-Computer-designed-board-game-wins-2012-Humies)
- Physics-puzzle PCG (Cut the Rope, Angry Birds) relies on simulated agents/random players for playability. Building a reliable agent is hard in continuous time/state. Experience models predicting challenge and frustration exist for Angry Birds, but no direct fun-validation player study was found. — [Ropossum AIIDE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12611); [Shaker 2015](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/files/226436460/Shaker2015Projection.pdf); [Stephenson & Renz 2016](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~jrenz/papers/stephenson-renz-aiide16.pdf)
- Difficulty metrics validated against people exist for daily puzzles: solve-time distributions (Puzzazz) and completion rates (Shortz). — [GeekWire 2016](https://www.geekwire.com/2016/hard-new-york-times-crossword-startup-finds-answer-first-ever-data-analysis/)
- Constrained MAP-Elites in MiniDungeons 2 used *simplicity* as quality, a reminder that "more stuff" is not "more fun". — [arXiv 1906.05175](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.05175)
- Clark 2009: near-miss motivation requires perceived control. This implies a measurable target, the proportion of failed shots that end "close" (see Inferences). — [PMC2658737](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2658737)

### Inferences (proposed metric suite for Last Orders: hypotheses, not validated)
Compute with a deterministic headless solver that samples shots on a (angle, power, spin, release-time) grid with Monte Carlo jitter:
1. **Solvability (hard constraint):** every circle reachable within the pour limit by a bot; trick-shot route exists for any intended trick. Reject otherwise (Ropossum-style).
2. **Precision band / leniency:** fraction of jittered shots that score, per customer. Map to a weekday target (wide on Monday, narrow on Saturday) and reject below a floor (pixel-perfect = frustrating).
3. **Naive-shot failure ("aha" proxy):** does the straight line-of-sight shot fail (blocked, hat, slap) while a bank/trick route succeeds? Target: at least one customer per map where yes. Compare: number of distinct solution *families* (cluster successful shots by contact sequence, e.g. "rail-L → circle" vs "direct"). 1-3 families = clear intended solution with some room for creativity; more than 5 = no puzzle; 0 = broken.
4. **Bounce count on solution paths:** mean contacts on the cheapest successful family. Moderate (1-2) is likely ideal. Track the distribution across days to avoid monotony.
5. **Mechanic usage vector:** fraction of successful shots that interact with each mechanic (rail, obstacle, slap arm, hat, spin necessity, trick). The "signature mechanic" should be used by most solutions that day (otherwise the twist is skippable).
6. **Near-miss density:** among failed jittered shots, the fraction ending within X cm of the circle edge. Higher means more "so close" motivating failures. Avoid maps where failures are binary (fall off/blocked far away).
7. **Path legibility:** angle between the intended bank line and visible geometry; whether the first rail contact point lies within the camera view. Plus a "telegraph" check that any hazard on the intended path is visible from the starting view.
8. **Novelty vs recent days:** distance in behaviour space (metrics 2-6 plus layout descriptors) to the previous N days. Require a minimum distance and a different signature mechanic from yesterday (anti-oatmeal).
9. **Simplicity/clutter:** object count and overlap. Penalise clutter (MiniDungeons simplicity result).
10. **Post-launch calibration:** log anonymised tries-to-success and completion per customer per day. Regress the above metrics against median tries, abandonment and (if available) an in-game 👍/👎 or "rate today's saloon" prompt. This is the Ludi/Puzzazz approach: correlate measured features with human ratings, keep the predictive ones.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found that validates specific metrics (bounce count, solution uniqueness, precision band) against *player-rated fun* for physics/aiming games. The suite above is extrapolated from Ludi (board games), daily-crossword difficulty data, and near-miss psychology.
- The specific 16 Ludi criteria were not retrieved (e.g. whether "drama"/"uncertainty" are among them).
