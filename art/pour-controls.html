Split the aim from the pour

Friends still found the pour too sensitive. The cause is that one finger does two jobs: the direction of the drag sets the aim and its length sets the strength. Any wobble while you pull for strength also swings the aim, and the other way round.

NOW

Drag back anywhere on the bar. Angle is aim, length is strength. Let go to pour.

PROPOSAL

Touch and drag on the bar only aims, and lifting the finger never pours. Strength gets its own control at the edge of the screen, the way 8 Ball Pool has a separate power stick.

The line points from the glass to your finger. Touch far up the bar for finer aim.

MY PICK

Pull the tap handle down to set strength and let go to pour. The full pull runs the whole height of the bar, about twice today's 240 units. Push it back to the top to cancel.

Hold to fill

Hold the button and the shot glass fills at a steady rate, full in 2 seconds. Let go to pour. Slide off the button to cancel. Nothing to drag, so a shaky thumb can't change anything.

Keg valve + bell

Turn the valve wheel to set strength. It stays where you leave it, so you can check it before you commit. Ring the bell to pour. The slowest option and the most exact, and a repeat pour is one tap.

Beer tap on desktop

The tap takes the place of the PULL meter between the info panel and the bar, full height, drawn at 2× so the pixels stay square on a normal monitor. With the mouse, aim on the bar and drag the knob down. With the keyboard, click the screen below, then use Q / E to aim and hold Space: the knob slides down by itself and you let go to pour.

Other things that could stand in for the cue

Bartender's armThe forearm already drawn behind the glass becomes a lever at the side: pull it back like a shuffleboard push. Same feel as A, but less clear as a control.
Slot-machine leverA saloon one-armed bandit on the wall. Fun, but pulling down to throw forward reads backwards.
Bell ropePull a rope hanging from the ceiling. Works like A, but a rope on the bar side hides the customers.
Swing meterA needle sweeps back and forth and you tap to stop it, golf-game style. It rewards timing instead of judgement and would make the game feel harder, not calmer.
